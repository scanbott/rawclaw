## 1. Brand Archetype: The Creator-Rebel

Raw Growth sits at the intersection of two archetypes:

**The Creator** — We build things. Tangible, measurable, operational infrastructure that compounds. We don't consult, we install. We don't advise, we deliver.

**The Rebel** — We reject the agency model, the retainer trap, the "strategy deck" industrial complex. We reject 6-month contracts, vague deliverables, and the assumption that business owners need to understand AI to benefit from it.

**Archetype expression:**
- Creator shows up in: product quality, specificity of deliverables, "you keep everything" ownership model
- Rebel shows up in: no contracts, calling out broken agency models, Chris's age/unconventional path, directness

## 2. Voice & Tone Guide

### Core Voice Attributes

| Attribute | Dial Setting | Description |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Formality | 3/10 | Casual, conversational, never corporate. Like texting a smart friend. |
| Confidence | 9/10 | Absolute certainty without arrogance. Quiet competence, not chest-beating. |
| Specificity | 10/10 | Always concrete. Numbers, timelines, deliverables. Never vague. |
| Warmth | 7/10 | Genuinely helpful, not transactional. Care about the outcome, not the sale. |
| Urgency | 4/10 | Never pressure. Let the logic create urgency naturally. |

### Tone Shifts by Context

| Context | Tone Adjustment |
|---------|----------------|
| Instagram captions | Peak casual. Short sentences. Line breaks. "bro" is fine. |
| LinkedIn posts | Slightly more structured. Still casual but professional-adjacent. |
| Sales calls | Warm, curious, zero pressure. Ask more than you tell. |
| Email sequences | Direct, value-first. Feels like a note from a friend, not a newsletter. |
| Website copy | Confident, clean, premium. Let the product speak. |
| Case studies | Data-forward, let results do the talking. Minimal editorializing. |

### Do's and Don'ts

**DO:**
- Use "we install" / "we build" / "we deploy" (active, tangible verbs)
- Reference specific numbers: "60+ pieces of content per week", "8 trained agents", "7-90 days"
- Speak to the outcome the buyer cares about (time back, headcount savings, compounding growth)
- Use casual language naturally: "look", "here's the thing", "honestly"
- Demonstrate before you describe — show the dashboard, the output, the agent working
- Say "you keep everything" early and often

**DON'T:**
- Use buzzwords: "synergy", "leverage", "unlock", "disrupt", "revolutionize", "cutting-edge"
- Hype AI capabilities beyond what the product actually does
- Use scarcity tactics: "only 3 spots left", "price going up at midnight"
- Sound corporate: "we're pleased to announce", "our proprietary methodology"
- Trash-talk competitors by name
- Use emojis excessively (1-2 per post max, never in professional contexts)
- Say "AI-powered" as if that phrase alone means anything

### Example Rewrites

| Before (bad) | After (on-brand) |
|---|---|
| "Unlock the power of AI to revolutionize your business operations" | "We install 8 AI agents that do the work of a $150K hire. You keep everything." |
| "Our proprietary AI technology leverages cutting-edge machine learning" | "Your AI sales manager follows up with every lead within 60 seconds. No days off." |
| "Schedule a call to learn more about our transformative solutions" | "Want to see what 60 pieces of content per week looks like? I'll show you a live demo — no pitch." |
| "Limited spots available! Don't miss out on this opportunity!" | "We take on 3-4 new installs per month. If the timing works, let's talk." |
| "We're excited to partner with forward-thinking businesses" | "We build AI departments for businesses doing $1M-$15M. Month-to-month. No BS." |

## 3. Core Messaging Pillars

### Pillar 1: "Install, Don't Hire"
**Core idea:** An AI department is infrastructure you own, not a service you rent.

Supporting proof points:
- 8 trained AI agents replace $150-250K/yr in headcount
- Installed in 7-90 days (vs. 3-6 months to hire and train a team)
- No contract, month-to-month — you keep everything if you leave
- Works alongside your existing tools (not a rip-and-replace)
- Compounds: gets smarter every month vs. employee learning curves

### Pillar 2: "Compounding Infrastructure"
**Core idea:** Most agencies extract value. We build assets that accumulate.

Supporting proof points:
- Content engine produces 60+ pieces per week that build SEO, brand, and audience over time
- AI sales manager improves conversion patterns based on historical data
- Company hub centralizes institutional knowledge (never lost when someone quits)
- Flywheel architecture: each agent feeds the next (content feeds leads, leads feed sales, sales feeds data)
- After month 6, the system does more than it did in month 1 — without additional cost

### Pillar 3: "Ownership, Not Dependency"
**Core idea:** We're the opposite of an agency that holds your stuff hostage.

Supporting proof points:
- You keep all agents, dashboards, templates, workflows when you leave
- No contract — if it's not working, leave tomorrow
- We train your team to manage the system (knowledge transfer built in)
- Your data stays in your accounts, your tools, your infrastructure
- We succeed when you don't need us anymore

### Pillar 4: "Demonstrate, Don't Describe"
**Core idea:** We show the product working before we ask for a dime.

Supporting proof points:
- Chris's content shows live agent demos, real dashboards, actual outputs
- Sales calls include live product walkthroughs (not slide decks)
- Free value in DMs: quick audits, teardowns, specific suggestions
- Client results shown with real numbers, not vague testimonials
- The product sells itself when people see it

## 4. Brand Story Arc

### Act 1: The Content Creator (origin)
Chris West, 21, starts creating content on Instagram. Learns what actually works by doing it himself — not from courses, not from books. Builds a following by being genuinely helpful and demonstrating results. Realizes: the content game is a volume game, and volume takes headcount.

### Act 2: The AI Discovery (catalyst)
Chris discovers that AI agents can produce content, manage sales follow-up, and centralize operations at a fraction of the cost. Builds an AI department for his own business first. Sees the compounding effect firsthand. The 8 agents do the work of 5-6 people. And they never take PTO.

### Act 3: The Installation Model (differentiation)
Instead of becoming another AI agency selling retainers and deliverables, Chris inverts the model: install the AI department inside the client's business. No lock-in. No hostage-taking. Month-to-month. You own everything. This becomes Raw Growth.

### Act 4: The Scaling Infrastructure (current)
Now installing AI departments for $1M-$15M online businesses. The product is refined: 8 agents, 7-90 day install, flywheel architecture. The bet: businesses that install AI infrastructure now will compound so far ahead of competitors that the gap becomes permanent.
