## 2. Competitor Profiles

### 2.1 OpenClaw (Claude Claw Fork)

**Overview**: Open-source fork of Claude Claw. Positioned as an AI agent platform for businesses. Community-driven development.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pricing** | Open-source (free) + paid hosted version ($500-2K/mo estimated) |
| **Positioning** | "Build your own AI agents" — developer-focused |
| **ICP** | Technical founders, dev teams, SMBs with engineering resources |
| **Strengths** | Open source credibility, low cost, developer community, transparency |
| **Weaknesses** | Stability issues (known), requires technical skill to deploy, no done-for-you service, support is community-based |
| **ICP Overlap** | Low-medium. Their users are DIY; our clients want done-for-you. |
| **Where They Win** | Budget-conscious technical founders who want full control |
| **Where They Lose** | Non-technical CEOs, anyone who values reliability over cost, anyone who doesn't want to manage AI |

**Raw Growth Advantage**: "OpenClaw gives you the building blocks. We give you the finished building, with a full-time maintenance crew."

### 2.2 Anti-Gravity

**Overview**: AI agency positioning as a premium service provider. Higher-end market positioning.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pricing** | $25-50K+ projects (estimated), retainer models unclear |
| **Positioning** | Premium AI transformation for enterprises |
| **ICP** | Mid-market to enterprise ($10M+), established companies |
| **Strengths** | Professional brand, enterprise credibility, likely has case studies and team |
| **Weaknesses** | Potentially overpriced for SMBs, slower delivery (enterprise timelines), may not understand online business models |
| **ICP Overlap** | Medium. Upper end of our ICP overlaps with their lower end. |
| **Where They Win** | Enterprise deals, regulated industries, companies that need "big brand" on the invoice |
| **Where They Lose** | Speed (we deliver in 7-90 days), price (we're 50-70% cheaper), understanding of online business models |

**Raw Growth Advantage**: "Anti-Gravity builds AI for Fortune 500 timelines and budgets. We build AI departments for 7-9 figure online businesses — faster, leaner, and purpose-built for your model."

### 2.3 N8N Agencies (Multiple)

**Overview**: Agencies that build automation workflows using N8N (open-source automation platform). Dozens of small agencies doing this.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pricing** | $2-10K projects, $500-3K/mo retainers |
| **Positioning** | "We automate your workflows" — automation-focused, not AI-focused |
| **ICP** | Small businesses, solopreneurs, budget-conscious operators |
| **Strengths** | Low cost, visual workflow builder (easy to understand), large N8N community, quick turnaround |
| **Weaknesses** | N8N is automation, not AI. Limited intelligence — workflows are rigid, not adaptive. Can't handle unstructured tasks. Breaks when edge cases appear. |
| **ICP Overlap** | Low-medium. Their clients are typically smaller and more price-sensitive. |
| **Where They Win** | Simple, predictable workflows. Businesses that just need "if this, then that." |
| **Where They Lose** | Anything requiring judgment, learning, or handling unpredictable inputs. Complex multi-step processes. |

**Raw Growth Advantage**: "N8N agencies build automations. We build intelligence. An automation follows rules. An AI department makes decisions, handles exceptions, and gets smarter over time."

### 2.4 Generic AI Consultants

**Overview**: Individual consultants or small firms offering AI strategy, workshops, or custom development. Highly fragmented market.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pricing** | $150-500/hour consulting, $5-20K projects |
| **Positioning** | "I'll help you figure out AI for your business" |
| **ICP** | Anyone confused about AI, from startups to mid-market |
| **Strengths** | Low commitment (hourly), personalized attention, often deep technical knowledge |
| **Weaknesses** | Advice, not execution. Client still has to build/implement. No ongoing support. Results depend on individual consultant quality. |
| **ICP Overlap** | Medium. Many prospects talk to consultants before finding us. |
| **Where They Win** | Companies that just want advice, not implementation. Companies with internal dev teams. |
| **Where They Lose** | Companies that want results, not recommendations. Anyone who's been through a consulting engagement and didn't see ROI. |

**Raw Growth Advantage**: "Consultants give you a PDF. We give you a running AI department. We don't advise — we build, install, and manage."

### 2.5 Parker AI

**Overview**: AI agency with a focus on AI automation and agent deployment. Positioning as a modern AI services firm.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pricing** | $10-30K projects (estimated), unclear retainer model |
| **Positioning** | AI automation agency — builds custom AI solutions |
| **ICP** | Tech-forward businesses, SaaS companies, digital agencies |
| **Strengths** | Strong technical team, modern tech stack, good brand/website |
| **Weaknesses** | May be project-based (not ongoing retainer), unclear differentiation from generic AI agencies |
| **ICP Overlap** | Medium-high. Similar ICP, similar price range. |
| **Where They Win** | One-off AI projects, companies that want to own and manage internally |
| **Where They Lose** | Ongoing management, the "AI department" framing (ongoing service vs. project), companies that don't want to manage AI internally |

**Raw Growth Advantage**: "Parker builds AI projects. We install AI departments. The difference: a project ends. A department runs forever, gets smarter, and we manage it for you."

### 2.6 Utari

**Overview**: AI agent platform/agency. Newer entrant in the AI services space.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pricing** | Unclear, likely $5-15K range |
| **Positioning** | AI agent deployment for businesses |
| **ICP** | SMBs, digital businesses |
| **Strengths** | Focused on agents (aligned with market trend), newer/more agile |
| **Weaknesses** | Less established, likely fewer case studies, unclear track record |
| **ICP Overlap** | Medium. |
| **Where They Win** | Potentially lower price point, may be more nimble |
| **Where They Lose** | Track record, depth of implementation, ongoing management |

**Raw Growth Advantage**: "We're not just deploying agents. We're building an entire department — 8 specialized agents that work together, with ongoing optimization and management."

### 2.7 Freelance Claude Code / AI Developers

**Overview**: Individual developers on Upwork, Fiverr, or through personal networks who can build AI agents.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pricing** | $50-200/hour, $2-10K per project |
| **Positioning** | "I can build that for you" |
| **ICP** | Anyone with a specific AI task or project |
| **Strengths** | Cheapest option, highly customizable, can find niche expertise |
| **Weaknesses** | No reliability guarantee, single point of failure, no ongoing management, quality varies wildly, no business context |
| **ICP Overlap** | Low. Our clients don't want to manage freelancers. |
| **Where They Win** | One-off technical tasks, prototype/POC work, companies with technical PMs who can manage freelancers |
| **Where They Lose** | Production systems, ongoing reliability, business-level thinking, anything requiring coordination of multiple agents |

**Raw Growth Advantage**: "Hiring a freelancer to build AI is like hiring a contractor to build one room. We build the whole house, furnish it, and maintain it."

## 6. Competitive Intelligence Gathering Process

### Ongoing Monitoring
- **Monthly**: Check competitor websites for pricing/positioning changes
- **Weekly**: Monitor competitor social media for new case studies, claims, clients
- **As-needed**: After every lost deal, document why — what competitor won, why they won
- **Quarterly**: Update this document with new intelligence

### Sources
- Competitor websites and blogs
- LinkedIn posts from competitor founders/employees
- G2, Capterra, Trustpilot reviews (when available)
- Prospect feedback during sales calls ("we're also talking to...")
- Industry reports and market analyses
- AI community discussions (Reddit, Twitter, Discord)

### Lost Deal Debrief Questions
When we lose a deal to a competitor:
1. Which competitor did they choose?
2. What was the deciding factor? (Price? Speed? Feature? Trust?)
3. What could we have done differently?
4. Would they reconsider in the future? Under what conditions?

## 7. Competitive Positioning by ICP Segment

### Agencies ($3-15M)
- **Primary competitors**: N8N agencies, Parker AI
- **Our angle**: "We understand agency operations. Your AI department handles client onboarding, reporting, and communication — the three biggest agency time-sinks."
- **Differentiator**: Industry-specific agent configurations

### Coaching/Info Products ($1-10M)
- **Primary competitors**: Generic AI consultants, freelancers
- **Our angle**: "Your business runs on content and community. Your AI department handles lead follow-up, content repurposing, student support, and community management."
- **Differentiator**: Understanding of coaching business models and funnels

### SaaS/Tech ($3-15M)
- **Primary competitors**: Parker AI, OpenClaw, internal teams
- **Our angle**: "Even with technical teams, your AI department handles the non-engineering operational work: customer support, onboarding, reporting, sales ops."
- **Differentiator**: We complement their tech team, not replace it

### Professional Services ($1-10M)
- **Primary competitors**: Generic consultants, Anti-Gravity
- **Our angle**: "Your team's time is your product. Every hour they spend on admin is an hour not spent on billable client work."
- **Differentiator**: ROI is directly measurable (hours freed up x billable rate)
