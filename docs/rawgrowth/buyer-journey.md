# Raw Growth — Buyer Journey Map

## Document Purpose
Complete psychological journey mapping from first awareness through advocacy, with content and touchpoint strategy for each stage.

---

## 1. Journey Overview

```
UNAWARE → PROBLEM-AWARE → SOLUTION-AWARE → PRODUCT-AWARE → MOST-AWARE → CUSTOMER → ADVOCATE
   |           |               |                |              |           |           |
 "I'm fine"  "Something's    "AI could       "Raw Growth    "I need    "It's      "Everyone
              not working"    fix this"       does this"     THIS"      working"    needs this"
```

**Typical timeline**: 2-6 weeks from first touch to close. Fastest deals close in 7-10 days (high urgency). Longest take 60-90 days (enterprises, multiple stakeholders).

## 2. Stage 1: UNAWARE

### What They're Thinking
- "Business is running fine. We're growing."
- "AI is interesting but not relevant to my business right now."
- "My team handles everything. We don't need to change."

### What They're Feeling
- Comfortable, maybe slightly overwhelmed by workload but managing
- Mild curiosity about AI when they see headlines
- Confidence in current operations (potentially misplaced)

### Key Questions
- None — they're not asking questions about this problem yet

### Content Needed
- **Pattern interrupt content**: "Your competitor just replaced 3 employees with AI" (Instagram reels)
- **Curiosity hooks**: "What an AI department looks like inside a $5M agency" (short-form video)
- **Status-driven content**: "Why 8-figure businesses are hiring AI departments" (LinkedIn post)
- **Fear of missing out**: "The companies that don't adopt AI in 2026 will not exist in 2028"

### Touchpoints
- Instagram Reels (discovery via Explore page or hashtags)
- LinkedIn feed (shared by someone in their network)
- Podcast guest appearance (heard Raw Growth mentioned)
- Conference/event (met Chris in person)

### Conversion Trigger
Seeing a specific, concrete example of AI doing something they thought only humans could do — and realizing their competitors might already be doing it.

### Raw Growth Assets for This Stage
- Instagram Reels (1/day, pattern interrupt style)
- LinkedIn thought leadership posts
- Podcast guest appearances
- Partner content (other agencies sharing Raw Growth content)

## 3. Stage 2: PROBLEM-AWARE

### What They're Thinking
- "We're spending too much on labor for tasks that feel repetitive."
- "My team is drowning in admin work. The important stuff isn't getting done."
- "I keep hearing about AI but I don't know where to start."
- "We tried ChatGPT/Zapier/some tool and it didn't really work."

### What They're Feeling
- Frustration with operational inefficiency
- Anxiety about falling behind competitors
- Overwhelm — too many AI tools, no clear path
- Skepticism from past failed AI experiments

### Key Questions
- "How much time/money am I actually wasting on manual work?"
- "Is AI mature enough to handle real business operations?"
- "What would it even look like to use AI in my business?"
- "Am I the only one who hasn't figured this out yet?"

### Content Needed
- **Problem amplification**: "The hidden cost of manual operations in a $5M business" (blog post)
- **Identification content**: "5 signs your business needs an AI department" (carousel/reel)
- **Pain quantification**: "Calculate how much your team wastes on tasks AI could do" (calculator/tool)
- **Failed DIY stories**: "Why ChatGPT alone won't transform your business" (long-form content)

### Touchpoints
- Blog posts (SEO: "AI for agencies", "automating business operations")
- Lead magnet: "AI Department Assessment" (10-question quiz → personalized score)
- Retargeting ads (saw a reel, now sees educational content)
- Email nurture (subscribed to newsletter)

### Conversion Trigger
Quantifying the problem. When they calculate that manual processes cost them $15-30K/month in labor, the next stage becomes inevitable.

### Raw Growth Assets for This Stage
- AI Department Assessment lead magnet
- "Cost of Manual Operations" calculator
- Blog posts targeting pain-point keywords
- Email sequence: "The AI Gap" (3 emails educating on the problem)

## 4. Stage 3: SOLUTION-AWARE

### What They're Thinking
- "OK, AI can help. But what's the right approach?"
- "Should I hire an AI person, use a platform, or outsource?"
- "I've seen N8N, Make, custom GPTs — what actually works at scale?"
- "Done-for-you vs. done-with-you vs. DIY — what fits my business?"

### What They're Feeling
- Cautious optimism — they believe in the solution category
- Analysis paralysis — too many options, no clear winner
- Fear of choosing wrong — expensive mistake if they pick the wrong vendor
- Impatience — they want results but don't want to waste time on the wrong path

### Key Questions
- "What's the difference between an AI tool and an AI department?"
- "How long until I see ROI?"
- "What does implementation actually look like?"
- "What are the risks? Data security? Reliability?"

### Content Needed
- **Comparison content**: "AI Department vs. Hiring AI Engineers vs. DIY Tools — An Honest Breakdown" (blog/video)
- **Framework content**: "The 3 Types of AI Implementations (and which one fits your business)" (webinar/long video)
- **Case study teasers**: "How Dineline went from 3 manual processes to 8 AI agents in 30 days" (social proof)
- **Myth-busting**: "No, AI won't steal your data / replace your team / break your business"

### Touchpoints
- Webinar: "AI Department Demo Day" (monthly, 60 minutes)
- YouTube long-form content (deep dives on implementation)
- Comparison blog posts (SEO: "AI agency vs in-house AI team")
- Partner-hosted webinars/podcasts

### Conversion Trigger
Seeing a live demo of an AI department in action — watching it handle real tasks in real-time. The "holy shit, that actually works" moment.

### Raw Growth Assets for This Stage
- Monthly webinar (see Asset 13)
- YouTube deep-dive videos
- Comparison blog posts
- Solution-stage email sequence (3 emails: comparisons, frameworks, demo invite)

## 5. Stage 4: PRODUCT-AWARE

### What They're Thinking
- "Raw Growth seems legit. But is it right for MY business?"
- "I like the concept but $15-20K upfront is a big commitment."
- "Who else has done this? Where are the case studies?"
- "My business is different — will it work for [specific industry/use case]?"

### What They're Feeling
- Interest mixed with caution
- The pull of desire (imagining their business with AI) vs. the push of fear (what if it doesn't work)
- Need for social proof and validation
- Comparing Raw Growth to alternatives mentally

### Key Questions
- "What exactly do I get for $15-20K?"
- "What happens if it doesn't work?"
- "How is Raw Growth different from [competitor]?"
- "Can I talk to someone who's already done this?"
- "What does the timeline look like? When do I see results?"

### Content Needed
- **Case studies**: Detailed before/after with specific metrics (hours saved, money saved, processes automated)
- **Pricing transparency**: "Here's exactly what you get and what it costs" (remove ambiguity)
- **Comparison to competitors**: "Raw Growth vs. OpenClaw vs. hiring in-house" (battlecard content)
- **Risk reversal**: "Our guarantee: if we don't deliver X results in Y days, Z happens"
- **FAQ content**: Address every objection preemptively

### Touchpoints
- Discovery call with Chris (30 minutes, NEPQ-style)
- Pre-call email sequence (3 emails: what to expect, case study, FAQ)
- Website case study pages
- DM conversations (answering specific questions)
- Sales deck / pitch video

### Conversion Trigger
Speaking to Chris on a call and having him diagnose their specific business problems with precision. The feeling of "this person actually understands my business" breaks through the remaining skepticism.

### Raw Growth Assets for This Stage
- Sales call framework (see existing sales-call skill)
- Pre-call email sequence
- Case study library (Dineline + future clients)
- Competitor battlecard (see Asset 16)
- Pricing page with scope breakdown

## 6. Stage 5: MOST-AWARE

### What They're Thinking
- "I want to do this. I just need to justify it / get buy-in / find the timing."
- "What if I wait another month? Will it be cheaper? Better?"
- "I need to talk to my partner/co-founder/CFO."
- "Let me just check one more thing..."

### What They're Feeling
- Excitement about the transformation
- Fear of commitment (natural buyer's resistance)
- Urgency vs. procrastination tension
- Looking for permission to say yes

### Key Questions
- "Can we start with a smaller scope?"
- "What's the payment structure?"
- "When can we start?"
- "What do you need from us to get going?"

### Content Needed
- **Urgency triggers**: "Every day without AI costs you $X" (personalized to their business)
- **Permission content**: "Why smart CEOs invest in AI departments now, not later"
- **Social proof stacking**: Multiple case studies, testimonials, logos
- **Risk reversal**: Clear guarantee or trial period offer
- **Scarcity (if real)**: "We onboard 2 new clients per month" (capacity constraint)

### Touchpoints
- Follow-up call with Chris (address final objections)
- Proposal document (custom scope + pricing)
- WhatsApp/text messages (high-touch, personal)
- Partner/referrer nudge ("Hey, I went through it, totally worth it")

### Conversion Trigger
A specific urgency event: competitor just launched AI, they just lost a key employee, they calculated the cost of waiting, or Chris creates genuine urgency with a capacity constraint.

### Raw Growth Assets for This Stage
- Custom proposal template
- Objection handling scripts (see existing sales-call skill)
- Follow-up sequence (3 emails + 2 texts over 5 days)
- "Cost of Waiting" calculator
- Capacity constraint messaging ("2 slots remaining this month")

## 7. Stage 6: CUSTOMER

### What They're Thinking
- "Did I make the right decision?" (buyer's remorse, days 1-3)
- "When do I start seeing results?" (impatience, days 4-14)
- "This is actually working." (validation, days 15-30)
- "What else can we automate?" (expansion, days 30-90)

### What They're Feeling
- Initial anxiety → growing confidence → excitement → advocacy readiness
- Need for reassurance in first 2 weeks
- Delight when first AI agent delivers value
- Pride in being an early adopter

### Key Questions
- "Is everything on track?"
- "What metrics should I be watching?"
- "Can we add more agents / capabilities?"
- "How do I explain this to my team?"

### Content Needed
- **Onboarding excellence**: Clear timeline, milestones, expectations document
- **Win reporting**: Weekly/monthly "here's what your AI did" reports
- **Expansion content**: "5 additional automations most clients add after month 1"
- **Team training**: How to work alongside AI agents

### Touchpoints
- Onboarding kickoff call (day 1)
- Weekly check-in calls (first month)
- Monthly business review (ongoing)
- Slack/Discord channel (async support)
- Automated "win" notifications (AI completed X tasks, saved Y hours)

### Conversion Trigger (to Advocate)
First measurable ROI moment. When the client can point to a specific number: "AI saved us 40 hours this week" or "We processed 200 more orders without adding staff."

### Raw Growth Assets for This Stage
- Onboarding playbook (see Asset 15)
- Monthly business review template
- Win report automation
- Client success Slack channel
- Expansion menu (upsell opportunities)

## 9. Emotional Journey Map

```
Stage:     UNAWARE   PROBLEM   SOLUTION   PRODUCT   MOST-AWARE   CUSTOMER   ADVOCATE

Emotion:   Comfort → Anxiety → Hope → Cautious → Excitement → Relief → Pride
                     Fear      Curiosity  Optimism   + Doubt      Validation  Evangelism
                     FOMO      Overwhelm  Comparison              Delight     Loyalty

Energy:    Low ----→ Rising --→ Peak ---→ Dip -----→ Peak ------→ Settling → Sustained
                                          (analysis   (commitment
                                          paralysis)   energy)
```

### Critical Emotional Moments

1. **The Wake-Up Call** (Unaware → Problem-Aware): Something shakes their comfort. A competitor adopts AI. An employee quits. Revenue plateaus. This is NOT something Raw Growth controls — but Raw Growth content should be there when it happens.

2. **The "Aha" Moment** (Problem → Solution): They see a specific example of AI doing what their team does manually — and doing it faster/cheaper/better. This IS something Raw Growth controls: the webinar demo, the Instagram reel showing AI in action, the case study metrics.

3. **The Comparison Trap** (Solution → Product): They start comparing options and can get stuck. Raw Growth must make comparison easy (not complex) and position as the obvious best fit for their segment.

4. **The Courage Moment** (Most-Aware → Customer): The moment they commit. This requires removing all friction: payment plan, guarantee, clear next steps, capacity urgency.

5. **The Validation Moment** (Customer, Week 2-3): First real result. If this is strong, they become an advocate. If this is weak, they become a churn risk.

## 10. Friction Points and Resolutions

| Stage | Friction Point | Resolution |
|-------|---------------|------------|
| Problem-Aware | "AI is overhyped / won't work for me" | Show industry-specific examples; let them talk to a reference client |
| Solution-Aware | "Too many options, can't decide" | Clear comparison framework; position done-for-you as the only option for busy CEOs |
| Product-Aware | "Too expensive upfront" | Payment plan option; ROI calculator showing payback in 45-60 days |
| Product-Aware | "No testimonials/case studies" | Prioritize getting Dineline results documented; use "live build" as social proof |
| Product-Aware | "Data security concerns" | Technical security document; explain on-premise option (Mac Minis); NDA available |
| Most-Aware | "Need to think about it" | Create genuine urgency (capacity limit); follow-up sequence with new value each touch |
| Most-Aware | "Need partner/board approval" | Provide "internal pitch" document they can share; offer call with decision-makers |
| Customer | "It's not working fast enough" | Set clear expectations in onboarding; celebrate small wins weekly |

## 11. Time-in-Stage Estimates

| Stage | Typical Duration | Fast Track | Slow Track |
|-------|-----------------|------------|------------|
| Unaware → Problem-Aware | 1-4 weeks | 1 day (trigger event) | Never (some never convert) |
| Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware | 1-2 weeks | Same day (if content hits) | 4-8 weeks |
| Solution-Aware → Product-Aware | 1-2 weeks | 2-3 days (webinar → call) | 4-6 weeks |
| Product-Aware → Most-Aware | 3-7 days | Same call | 2-4 weeks |
| Most-Aware → Customer | 1-7 days | Same day (urgency) | 2-8 weeks |
| Customer → Advocate | 30-60 days | 14 days (fast win) | 90+ days |
| **Total Journey** | **6-12 weeks** | **2-3 weeks** | **4-6 months** |

### Acceleration Tactics
- **Warm referral**: Skips Unaware and Problem-Aware stages entirely. Starts at Solution or Product-Aware.
- **Live demo/webinar**: Can compress Solution → Product → Most-Aware into a single session.
- **Urgency event**: Competitor adoption, employee departure, funding round — accelerates every stage.
- **Partner selling**: Partner pre-qualifies and pre-frames. Prospect arrives at Product-Aware.
