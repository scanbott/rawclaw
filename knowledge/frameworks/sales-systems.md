# Sales Systems Frameworks

Pre-loaded methodology for sales calls, DMs, and pipeline management.

---

## The Core Principle

Sales isn't persuasion. It's qualification. Your job is to find the people who already have the problem, already want the solution, and just need to be shown that yours is the right one.

The best salespeople talk less and listen more.

---

## Discovery Call Framework

### The Opening (2-3 minutes)
Set the frame. You're both deciding if this is a fit.
"Thanks for jumping on. Here's how I like to run these -- I'll ask you some questions to understand where you're at, you'll get a sense of how we work, and by the end we'll both know if it makes sense to go further. Sound good?"

### The Situation (5-7 minutes)
Current state. Facts, not problems yet.
- "Tell me about the business -- what do you do, who do you serve?"
- "How long have you been at it?"
- "What does the team look like right now?"

### The Problem (7-10 minutes)
Go deeper. Find the real pain.
- "What's the main thing you're trying to solve right now?"
- "How long has this been going on?"
- "What have you tried so far? What happened?"
- "If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing, what would it be?"

### The Implication (5-7 minutes)
Make the pain real. Help them feel the cost of inaction.
- "What does that cost you -- in time, money, missed opportunities?"
- "If this isn't solved in the next 6 months, what happens?"
- "What's the thing you can't do right now because this isn't solved?"

### The Payoff (5-7 minutes)
Their desired outcome in their words.
- "If we could solve this, what would that make possible for you?"
- "What does success look like 6 months from now?"
- "Walk me through your ideal scenario."

### The Presentation (5-10 minutes)
Only after you've done the above. Show them specifically how you solve their specific problem. Reference what they said: "You mentioned X is the issue -- here's how we handle that."

### The Close
"Based on everything you've shared, I think we can help. The investment is [X]. What questions do you have before we move forward?"

Silence after the close. Let them talk next.

## Objection Handling

### "I need to think about it."
Root cause: they're not convinced of the value, or they're not the decision maker.
Response: "Of course. What's the main thing you want to think through?"
Then address that thing directly.

### "It's too expensive."
Root cause: they don't see the ROI, or they don't have the money.
Response: "Compared to what?" or "If this solved [the problem they named], what would that be worth to you?"
Don't discount. Explain the value.

### "I need to talk to my partner/team."
Response: "Of course. When you talk to them, what's the main thing you'll be sharing?"
Then: "Would it help to have me on a call with them to answer questions directly?"

### "We're not ready yet."
Root cause: fear of change or unclear ROI.
Response: "What would need to be true for you to feel ready?"
This surfaces the real objection.

### "Let me do more research."
Response: "What specifically are you trying to figure out? I might be able to save you time."

## DM Outreach Framework

### The Cold DM Structure
```
Line 1: Personalized hook (specific to them, not generic)
Line 2: The observation (what you noticed about their situation)
Line 3: The bridge (connect their situation to the outcome you help with)
Line 4: The soft CTA (a question, not a pitch)
```

Example:
"Saw your post about the client delivery bottleneck -- that's one of the most common things we see with agencies at your stage.

Curious -- is that mostly a team capacity issue or a systems issue for you?"

### Follow-Up Cadence
- Day 1: Initial DM
- Day 3: Value add (share something relevant, no ask)
- Day 7: Soft check-in
- Day 14: Final bump
- After that: move on. They'll reach out if they're ready.

## Pipeline Management

### Stage Definitions
1. **Lead** -- expressed interest, no conversation yet
2. **Contacted** -- had initial touchpoint
3. **Qualified** -- confirmed they have the problem and budget
4. **Proposal** -- proposal sent or call scheduled
5. **Negotiating** -- verbal yes, working on terms
6. **Won** -- signed and paid
7. **Lost** -- went elsewhere or not ready

### Weekly Pipeline Review
Every Monday:
- What moved stages last week?
- What's been stuck for 7+ days? Why?
- What needs a follow-up today?
- What should be archived (gone cold)?

### The One Rule
Every deal needs a clear next action and a date. If there's no next step, it's not a deal -- it's a wish.
