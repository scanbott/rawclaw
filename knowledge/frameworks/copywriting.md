# Copywriting Frameworks

Pre-loaded expert methodology. Load this before writing any copy.

---

## The Fundamental Rule

Copy doesn't sell products. Copy sells the transformation. The product is just the mechanism.

Lead with their world (problem, desire, frustration). Then introduce your mechanism as the bridge to the outcome they want.

---

## The Four U's (Headline Evaluation)

Every headline should score high on:
1. **Useful** -- does it promise a benefit?
2. **Urgent** -- is there a reason to read now?
3. **Unique** -- does it say something no one else says?
4. **Ultra-specific** -- does it have a number, name, or concrete detail?

Score each headline 1-4 on each U. Aim for 12+ out of 16.

## Proven Headline Formulas

### The How-To
"How to [achieve desired outcome] without [feared sacrifice]"
"How to [specific result] even if [objection]"

### The Secret
"The [adjective] secret to [desired outcome]"
"What [authority] knows about [topic] that you don't"

### The Warning
"Warning: Don't [action] until you [read this / do this]"
"Stop [common advice] -- here's why it's making things worse"

### The Social Proof
"How [type of person] went from [before] to [after] in [timeframe]"
"[Number] [type of person] are doing [thing]. Here's why."

### The Curiosity Gap
"The [topic] mistake that costs [ICP] [amount/time] every year"
"What nobody tells you about [common topic]"

## The Lead Paragraph

The lead does ONE job: make them read the next sentence.

**The Narrative Lead:** Start in the middle of a story. Tension first, context second.
"It was 11pm on a Tuesday when I realized I was about to lose everything I'd built."

**The Problem Lead:** Name the pain they feel daily. Be specific.
"If you're running a consulting firm and you still spend Sundays writing proposals, this is for you."

**The Counterintuitive Lead:** Challenge what they believe.
"The reason most agencies can't scale isn't their team, their clients, or their pricing. It's their systems."

**The Big Promise Lead:** State the transformation directly.
"What I'm about to show you will cut your proposal time from 4 hours to 20 minutes."

## The Body: Bucket Brigade

Keep them reading with transition phrases that create momentum:
- "Here's the thing..."
- "But that's not all."
- "Now here's where it gets interesting."
- "The reason most people miss this is..."
- "Let me show you exactly what I mean."
- "In fact..."
- "Which means..."
- "And here's why that matters."

---

## Proof Hierarchy (Strongest to Weakest)

1. Specific results with numbers ("From $0 to $47K/month in 6 months")
2. Named testimonials with specific outcomes
3. Case studies (before → action taken → after)
4. Third-party validation (press, certifications)
5. Social proof (number of customers)
6. Logic / reasoning
7. Your own claims (weakest -- avoid leaning on these)

Always lead with your strongest proof.

---

## The Close

Three elements every close needs:
1. **Restate the transformation** (remind them what they're getting)
2. **Address the risk** (guarantee, reversal, what happens if they don't act)
3. **Create urgency** (genuine -- deadline, limited spots, price increase)

Never invent urgency. If there's no real reason to act now, make the next step as frictionless as possible instead.

---

## Editing Rules (Read Every Draft Against These)

1. Delete the first paragraph (you usually need to earn into it)
2. Every sentence: does it earn the next? If not, cut it.
3. Replace every adjective with a number or name
4. Find every passive sentence and make it active
5. Shorten every paragraph by 30% -- you always have more than needed
6. Read aloud -- if you breathe awkwardly, rewrite that sentence
7. Would your ICP forward this to someone? If not, find out why.
